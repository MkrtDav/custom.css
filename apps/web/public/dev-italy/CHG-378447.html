Infrastructure: EEA hosting, recovery, monitoring and the ADM link

What ADM's technical rules require of the hosting, data separation, recovery, monitoring, logs, time source and the link to ADM's central system.

Use with the task CHG-378447. The task is the requirement. This kit adds reference detail for building and testing it: ADM rules with their sources, flows, messages and checks. If the kit and the task differ, the task prevails; if the task and ADM's protocol differ, the protocol prevails.

At a glance

  • Hosting. All infrastructure, cloud included, is in the EEA, and ADM can inspect it.
  • Data separation. The gaming account system holds only the concessionaire's data; game-system data is separated at least logically per concessionaire.
  • Recovery. Every component is redundant; disaster recovery replicates in real time, or at most 120 seconds after a change, to a geographically separate site.
  • Monitoring and logs. Automated monitoring, audit logs on every component, one UTC time source.
  • Link to ADM. A dedicated line or a VPN over the internet, redundant, in the concessionaire's name, for gaming data only.
  • Messages to ADM are in CHG-375322 to CHG-375326. Record retention and ADM data access are in CHG-375897.

Rules

A. Hosting and data

  1. All infrastructure resources of the operator system, including cloud solutions, are located within the European Economic Area. Keep all hardware and software infrastructure, especially servers, for the conceded activities in Italy or another EEA country, with all information ADM needs for supervision fully available. Technical rules Part 2 ch. 1; Concession agreement Art. 7(1)(g); D.Lgs. 41/2024 Art. 6(5)(n)
  2. Any cloud solution meets the requirements of AgID and the National Cybersecurity Agency (ACN) for qualification as a service to the Public Administration and appears in ACN's catalogue of qualified cloud services. (Should be checked with the partner: whether our hosting is qualified and listed, and what the lab accepted for Netbet's current platform.) Technical rules Part 2 ch. 1; Guidelines 2.1.7
  3. Guarantee digital data sovereignty, complying with the rules on data residency, data transfer, data access and data security; data are encrypted in transit and at rest. ADM fixes no algorithm or key length. Technical rules Part 2 ch. 1
  4. Prevent data loss, damage, theft or compromise and service interruption, including through proper placement and protection of equipment against environmental threats and unauthorised access. Technical rules Part 2 ch. 1
  5. The gaming account system holds only the concessionaire's own data; data structures with data from several concessionaires are not allowed. Each game system's data, including accounting, is at least logically separated per concessionaire, including separate runtime processes for the client side of game applications. Both a dedicated database and a dedicated schema with logical separation are accepted. Guidelines 1.2
  6. Game systems a service-provider concessionaire offers to other concessionaires are physically or logically separated where the game type allows, so the data of each concessionaire can always be isolated. Unless built on cloud computing, a service-provider concessionaire's infrastructure is not shared with other service-provider concessionaires. Technical rules Part 2 ch. 6; Guidelines 2.6.1

B. Architecture and availability

  1. Build and operate the operator system in line with the technical specifications of the Technical Rules. Technical rules Part 1 par. 1.1(1)
  2. The operator system comprises: game systems (game platforms with game applications, and a game acceptance system); the presentation system (website and apps); the gaming account system; the accounting system; the monitoring and control system, including automated monitoring of hardware and software; and the telematic network. Technical rules Part 2 ch. 1
  3. The architecture provides the highest assurance of capacity, availability, scalability, performance, security and controllability, with redundancy of every component and recovery without data loss. Document redundant backup for every critical component (servers, databases, networks), a monitoring and alarm system confirming that the high-availability mechanisms work, and emergency plans with trained staff. Technical rules Part 2 ch. 1; Guidelines 2.1.8
  4. Build each function with modular development techniques, so every component can be identified with certainty: independent modules with well-defined functions, source code that separates components and uses unique full file names, and documentation mapping functions to modules. Technical rules Part 2 ch. 1; Guidelines 2.1.9
  5. Disaster recovery keeps all information traceable and reconstructable up to the moment of the disaster: data replicated in real time, or at most every 120 seconds after creation or change on the primary database, with the replica stored at a geographically separate site. The 120 seconds is a replication limit, not a recovery-time target. Technical rules Part 1 par. 1.1(2)
  6. Apply documented backup policies. The document covers: owners of the process, backup classification and criticality, retention period, reliability of procedures and devices, media protection, testing of backup effectiveness, regulatory compliance and adequacy of documentation. Technical rules Part 2 ch. 1; Guidelines 2.1.12

C. Monitoring, logs, malfunctions, time

  1. Provide a monitoring and control system, including automated monitoring, of the hardware and software infrastructure, separate from the software-integrity check. Technical rules Part 2 ch. 1
  2. Keep audit logs that record chronologically the activities of operators and administrators on every component, with date, time, operation type and user identity. Logs are complete and easy to search, protected against alteration and unauthorised access, and can be queried by the monitoring system; use error logs to take corrective action. Technical rules Part 2 ch. 1; Guidelines 2.1.11
  3. When malfunctions or deviations from the certified system occur, promptly identify the cause with the monitoring tools and document the technical problem, how it was handled and how correct operation was restored. Technical rules Part 1 par. 3
  4. If the malfunction does not stop gaming, fix the problem and remove the anomaly as soon as possible, including by changing the system. Technical rules Part 1 par. 3
  5. If the malfunction stops gaming, the operator system runs the integrity-check procedures, and the concessionaire notifies the affected players and ADM; any full refund of bets is at the concessionaire's expense. Technical rules Part 1 par. 3
  6. All components of the operator system use UTC as the time reference, synchronised from a single UTC time source using standard protocols (for example NTP); logs and message timestamps refer to that source. Technical rules Part 2 ch. 1; Guidelines 2.1.5
  7. Protect the integrity of components and information with automatic mechanisms; automated integrity checks block the affected component when a check fails. If a check fails or cannot compare against the expected value, record date and time and let the component run temporarily; if it fails again after 15 calendar days, block it. Store each check result, queryable by the monitoring system. Technical rules Part 2 ch. 1; Guidelines 2.1.13
  8. Any software the player must install for the site or apps to work must prevent data loss, damage, theft or compromise, reduce the risk from malicious code, and connect only to the operator system's components. Site and apps communicate with the operator system only over secure protocols with authentication certificates. Technical rules Part 2 ch. 7; Guidelines 2.7.8

D. Link to ADM

  1. Build and operate the operator's own telematic network and the communication with the ADM central system, exchanging all the information the Technical Rules and protocols specify electronically, continuously and in real time, over a network dedicated exclusively to gaming data. The gaming account system connects to the central system through the concessionaire's own direct link, even when a service-provider's game system is used. Technical rules Part 1 par. 1.1(3); Part 2 ch. 2, 5, 8; Guidelines 2.8.1, 2.9.6; Concession agreement Art. 15(3)
  2. Telematic network connections are registered in the concessionaire's name, use market-standard technology from telecom operators, apply security that ensures the integrity and confidentiality of exchanged data, and keep service continuous during gaming under any system load. Technical rules Part 2 ch. 8
  3. Any proprietary protocol used between operator-system components has characteristics equivalent to those required for transport on the telematic network. Technical rules Part 2 ch. 8
  4. Links between the border equipment and the central system site are either dedicated LAN-to-LAN FastEthernet up to 100 Mbps, or a VPN over the internet using the specified technologies; links are compatible with the central system and redundant. Technical rules Part 2 ch. 8
  5. An SPC (Public Connectivity System) link, where available, may be used only for horse-race betting and only within Italy. Technical rules Part 2 ch. 8
  6. Monitor the telematic network continuously against external threats; protect systems, applications and data in transit against incomplete transmission, misrouting and attacks of any kind, keeping messages intact and confidential. Technical rules Part 2 ch. 8; Concession agreement Art. 10(4)
  7. (Should be checked with the partner: how signing certificates and keys for the ADM messages are issued, and whether Digitain may hold keys for certificates issued in Netbet's name.)
  8. (Should be checked with the partner: ADM's rules use these terms without a measurable value: "highest assurance", fixing a malfunction "as soon as possible", "constant" monitoring of the link, logs that are "complete" and "searchable". The values are validated with Netbet and the lab. Service levels are in the SLA task.)
